Union Rules for Crew Welfare in Video Production

Union regulations established by organizations such as the International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees (IATSE) and the Screen Actors Guild-American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA) are designed to safeguard the health, safety, and working conditions of video production crews and talent. These agreements establish legally binding standards for maximum work hours, mandatory rest periods, meal provision, workplace safety, and protection against harassment. Adhering to these rules is mandatory for signatory productions to ensure safe and sustainable environments on set.

Mandatory Rest Periods and Turnaround Times

Turnaround times are strictly enforced to prevent extreme fatigue and dangerous commutes.

  • Daily Turnaround: Both IATSE and SAG-AFTRA generally require a minimum 10- to 12-hour rest period between dismissal ("wrap") and the next day’s call time. Under SAG-AFTRA agreements, performers typically must receive 12 consecutive hours of rest.
  • Weekly Rest: Crews are entitled to regular weekly rest periods—typically 54 consecutive hours for a five-day work week and 32 consecutive hours for a six-day work week.
  • Penalties: If a production violates turnaround limits (known as a "forced call"), the producer must pay substantial penalty rates to the affected crew members or cast until proper rest is provided.

Maximum Hours and Overtime Regulations

Unions enforce escalating pay scales to discourage excessively long working days that compromise physical and cognitive functioning.

  • Overtime Accrual: A standard day is usually based on an 8-hour or 10-hour guarantee. Work extending beyond these hours incurs time-and-a-half or double-time pay.
  • "Golden Time": In many IATSE contracts, work extending past 14 or 16 hours transitions into "Golden Time," where crew members are compensated at double or triple their base rate for every hour worked until wrap.

Meal Breaks and Nutritional Provision

To maintain crew energy and well-being, strict rules govern when and how workers are fed on set.

  • Six-Hour Rule: The first meal must be served within six hours from general call time, and subsequent meals must be served within six hours of the end of the previous meal.
  • Meal Penalties: Missing or delaying a meal triggers meal penalties, which accrue automatically for every half-hour or fraction thereof that a meal is delayed.
  • Continuous Craft Services: Productions must provide continuous access to fresh water, coffee, and substantial snacks throughout the duration of the shooting day.

Environmental and Physical Safety Protocols

Crew welfare mandates require producers to mitigate environmental and physical hazards on set.

  • Safety Bulletins: IATSE adheres to Industry Safety Bulletins issued by the Contract Services Administration Trust Fund (CSATF), covering guidelines for working with pyrotechnics, firearms, elevated platforms, and extreme weather (heat and cold).
  • Right of Refusal: Union members possess the contractual right to refuse tasks they deem imminently dangerous without facing disciplinary action or termination.
  • Stunt and Water Safety: SAG-AFTRA requires qualified, dedicated coordinators for stunts and scenes involving open water, ensuring adequate medical personnel and rescue assets are stationed on set.

Harassment Prevention and Dignity on Set

Crew welfare extends beyond physical safety to psychological well-being and respectful treatment.

  • Intimacy Coordinators: For scenes involving simulated sex, nudity, or hyper-exposed physical vulnerability, SAG-AFTRA mandates or strongly protects the inclusion of certified intimacy coordinators.
  • Reporting Mechanisms: Both IATSE and SAG-AFTRA maintain clear protocols and dedicated safety hotlines that allow members to anonymously report harassment, discrimination, or hazardous working conditions without fear of retaliation.
