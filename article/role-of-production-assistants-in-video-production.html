Role of Production Assistants in Video Production

Production assistants (PAs) serve as the logistical backbone of any professional video shoot, performing the vital operational tasks and protocol enforcement needed to keep a shoot on schedule. By bridging communication gaps between department heads, managing physical logistics, and enforcing set etiquette, PAs allow directors, cinematographers, and specialized crews to focus entirely on their creative and technical responsibilities.

Managing Daily Operations and Logistics

PAs keep a production moving forward by handling the essential day-to-day administrative and physical tasks:

  • Call Sheet and Paperwork Distribution: PAs ensure cast and crew receive updated call sheets, sides, and schedules. They also manage critical administrative paperwork, such as collecting signed talent release forms, NDA agreements, and crew timecards.
  • Gear and Supply Runs: When unexpected needs arise—such as replacing broken expendables, sourcing specific props, or retrieving specialized camera gear—PAs are dispatched on runs to ensure delays do not halt the shooting schedule.
  • Departmental Support: PAs are often assigned to assist key departments. A camera PA might carry lenses and batteries, an art PA helps redress a background, and a wardrobe PA organizes racks and steamers.
  • Staging and Staging Maintenance: PAs organize holding areas for background actors, set up video villages for directors and clients, and manage craft services to ensure cast and crew remain fueled and hydrated throughout long production hours.

Maintaining Set Discipline and Protocol

A chaotic set costs time and money. PAs play a frontline role in maintaining the professional decorum and safety standards necessary for a functional set:

  • Executing Lockdowns: During a take, PAs execute "lockdowns" (or "lockups") at entryways, stairwells, and perimeter lines. They physically block foot traffic, stop non-crew members from entering the frame, and prevent outside disruptions that could ruin a take.
  • Echoing Assistant Director (AD) Cues: PAs act as acoustic relays across large locations. When the First AD calls "Rolling," "Quiet on set," or "Cut," PAs repeat these commands across the stage or location to ensure everyone—especially background talent and distant crew members—hears and reacts instantly.
  • Enforcing Radio Protocol: PAs monitor and manage walkie-talkie etiquette, ensuring communication remains strictly professional, concise, and routed to the correct production channels.
  • Site Safety and Cleanliness: PAs continually scan the set for safety hazards, identifying loose cables, unflagged obstacles, and blocked emergency exits. They also maintain set hygiene by conducting ongoing trash sweeps, ensuring the location remains clean and ready for work at all times.
