Essential Insurance Riders for Rented Video Gear

Renting high-end cameras, specialty lenses, and advanced lighting packages is standard practice in video production, but standard general liability policies rarely protect these assets. To prevent catastrophic out-of-pocket costs, production companies must secure specific equipment insurance riders tailored to rented property. This guide outlines the essential insurance endorsements and riders required to fully cover expensive rented production gear against physical damage, theft, operational downtime, and logistical risks.

Rented Equipment Floater (Inland Marine)

The rented equipment floater is the foundational rider required by virtually every rental house. Unlike standard commercial property insurance, which only covers equipment at a fixed business address, an Inland Marine floater covers gear that moves between locations, sets, and stages.

This rider covers the actual physical loss or damage to equipment you do not own. When binding this policy, the limit must reflect the total replacement value of all rented gear on your shoot simultaneously, rather than its depreciated cash value.

Loss of Use Endorsement

If rented gear is damaged or stolen, the financial liability extends beyond repair or replacement costs. Rental houses lose ongoing revenue while waiting for replacement items to arrive or repairs to finish.

A Loss of Use rider pays the rental house for the lost rental income during this downtime. Without this endorsement, the production company is personally liable for paying the daily or weekly rental rate of the equipment until it is returned to inventory in working condition.

Voluntary Parting and False Pretenses

Standard theft coverage requires evidence of forced entry or violent taking. It specifically excludes situations where gear is voluntarily handed over to someone who turns out to be a scammer, an unauthorized sub-renter, or an imposter claiming to be a crew member.

A Voluntary Parting rider (often called "False Pretenses" coverage) protects the production if equipment is lost due to fraud, trickery, or unauthorized conversion by third parties.

In-Transit and Unattended Vehicle Rider

Camera packages and grip trucks spend significant time on the road, where standard property coverage often lapses. An In-Transit rider protects gear damaged during transport due to collisions or rough handling.

Additionally, you must verify that this rider includes coverage for unattended vehicle theft. Many standard policies exclude theft from vehicles unless strict conditions are met, such as evidence of forced entry into a locked, windowless vehicle compartment.

Worldwide and Geographic Territory Extensions

Most domestic production insurance policies only cover gear within the United States and Canada. If your production crosses international borders, you must secure a territory extension endorsement. This rider ensures your rented equipment floater remains active regardless of the jurisdictions in which you film, cross customs, or stage your shoot.

Flood and Earthquake Extensions

Natural disasters are universally excluded from baseline Inland Marine policies. If you are shooting on location in areas prone to seismic activity or flash flooding, you must add specific earthquake and flood endorsements. Without these riders, water damage from storms or structural collapse during tremors will result in a total denial of the equipment claim.
