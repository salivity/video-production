Key Terms in a Video Production Crew Deal Memo

A crew deal memo is a legally binding short-form contract that defines the working relationship between an independent video production company and a hired crew member. This document outlines the financial and logistical expectations of a hire before production begins, ensuring both parties are protected against misunderstandings. The standard terms included in an independent video production crew deal memo cover compensation structures, work hours, intellectual property rights, credits, and termination policies.

1. Identification of Parties and Role

The deal memo begins by identifying the basic details of the engagement:

  • Production Company Information: The legal name of the entity producing the project.
  • Project Title: The working title or official name of the video or film.
  • Crew Member Information: Legal name, contact details, tax identification/Social Security Number, and loan-out company details (if applicable).
  • Job Title and Department: The specific position being hired (e.g., Director of Photography, Gaffer, Sound Mixer, Production Assistant).

2. Term and Dates of Employment

This section defines the timeframe and schedule of the job:

  • Effective Dates: The official start and anticipated completion dates.
  • Type of Days: A breakdown of prep days, shoot days, travel days, and wrap days.
  • Locations: Primary filming locations and reporting addresses.

3. Compensation and Overtime

Payment terms must be precise to prevent wage disputes:

  • Base Rate: The agreed-upon rate, typically structured as a daily rate based on an 8-, 10-, or 12-hour guarantee. Independent productions must still comply with applicable minimum wage laws.
  • Overtime Structure: The rate multiplier (e.g., 1.5x after 8 or 10 hours, 2x after 12 or 14 hours) applied to hours worked beyond the daily guarantee.
  • Kit/Gear Rental: A separate fee allocated for any personal equipment, tools, or vehicles provided by the crew member.
  • Payment Schedule: When and how payments are disbursed (e.g., weekly, Net 15, Net 30, or via a specific payroll processing company).

4. Working Conditions and Meal Penalties

Independent productions require clear boundaries regarding crew welfare:

  • Turnaround Time: The mandatory rest period between the wrap of one day and the call time of the next (typically 10 to 12 hours). Violations often trigger "forced call" penalty pay.
  • Meal Breaks: The requirement to provide a meal break every six hours from the general call time.
  • Travel and Mileage: Compensation for travel outside a designated local production zone (often a 30-mile radius), including per-mile vehicle reimbursement at standard government rates.
  • Per Diem: A daily stipend provided to cover meals and incidentals when working on distant locations.

5. Work-for-Hire and Intellectual Property

This clause is legally essential for the producer to secure ownership of the project:

  • Work-Made-for-Hire: Language stating that all creative work, footage, sound, designs, and materials produced by the crew member are considered "works made for hire" under copyright law.
  • Assignment of Rights: A fallback clause ensuring that if the work is not legally deemed a work-for-hire, the crew member irrevocably assigns all global rights, titles, and interests to the production company.

6. Screen Credit

The agreement establishes how the crew member will be publicly recognized:

  • Credit Placement: The exact wording and placement of the screen credit (e.g., in the main titles or the rolling end credits).
  • Conditions: Clauses stating that credits are subject to customary industry exclusions and that accidental failure to provide credit does not constitute a breach of contract giving rise to injunctive relief.

7. Confidentiality and Social Media

To prevent project leaks, deal memos include non-disclosure provisions:

  • Confidentiality: Prohibits sharing scripts, storylines, cast information, budget details, or proprietary methods.
  • Social Media Restrictions: Establishes rules regarding when and if crew members may post behind-the-scenes photos, videos, or set locations before the official release of the project.

8. Cancellation and Termination

This section addresses what happens if the schedule changes or the working relationship fails:

  • Cancellation/Kill Fee: Outlines what portion of the agreed rate is owed to the crew member if the production cancels shoot dates on short notice (e.g., within 24 to 48 hours).
  • Termination for Cause: Grounds for immediate dismissal without full payout, including misconduct, intoxication, breach of contract, or safety violations.
  • Force Majeure: Provisions defining payment and scheduling obligations in the event of unforeseen disasters, severe weather, or acts of God that halt production.

9. Insurance and Liability

  • Equipment Liability: Specifies whether the production company's insurance covers rented or personal gear brought by the crew member against damage or theft.
  • Workers' Compensation: Clarifies that the crew member is covered by the production’s workers' compensation policy in the event of an on-set injury.
