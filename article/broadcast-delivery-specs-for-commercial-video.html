Broadcast Delivery Specs for Commercial Video

Digital delivery specifications for network broadcast deliverables establish strict technical standards to ensure commercial spots air without technical defects, audio discrepancies, or formatting errors. This article outlines the essential technical requirements for commercial video production, covering file containers, video codecs, audio loudness compliance, layout structures, and quality control procedures required by major broadcast networks.

File Formats and Codecs

Broadcast networks require standardized wrappers and mezzanine-quality codecs that preserve image integrity while maintaining compatibility with automation and playback servers.

  • Container: MXF (Material Exchange Format) OP1a or QuickTime (.MOV).
  • Video Codecs:
    • ProRes 422 HQ: Standard for QuickTime wrappers.
    • Avid DNxHD / DNxHR: Standard for Avid-based broadcast pipelines.
    • XDCAM HD422 (MPEG-2): Standard at 50 Mbps within an MXF wrapper for many automated playout networks.
  • Resolution: Standard 1080i (interlaced) or 1080p (progressive) at 1920x1080. UHD/4K (3840x2160) is increasingly accepted or requested for specialized networks.
  • Frame Rates: 29.97 fps (interlaced as 59.94i) or 23.98/24 fps with standard 3:2 pulldown in North America (NTSC territories). 25 fps is standard in PAL territories.
  • Color Space: Rec. 709 standard dynamic range (SDR). High Dynamic Range (HDR) spots typically require Rec. 2020 using HLG or PQ transfer functions, depending on the network.

Audio Standards and Loudness Compliance

Audio standards are governed by federal legislation to prevent commercials from airing significantly louder than adjacent programming.

  • Loudness Regulations:
    • United States: ATSC A/85 compliance under the CALM Act requires an integrated target loudness of -24 LKFS/LUFS (±1.0 LUFS).
    • Europe/International: EBU R128 standard requires an integrated target loudness of -23 LUFS (±0.5 LUFS).
  • True Peak Limits: Maximum True Peak must not exceed -2.0 dBFS (or -1.0 dBFS on select networks).
  • Format: Uncompressed Linear PCM, 24-bit (or 16-bit), sampled at 48 kHz.
  • Channel Configuration:
    • Channels 1 & 2: Complete stereo mix (Left/Right).
    • Channels 3 through 8 (if 5.1 Surround is provided): Left, Right, Center, LFE, Left Surround, Right Surround.
    • Secondary Audio Programming (SAP) or Audio Description tracks may occupy channels 9 and 10 when required.

Slates, Timing, and Layout

Broadcast commercials require specific header framing to allow playout operators and automated ingest systems to identify and cue the asset.

  • Slate: A static graphic displaying metadata including the Ad-ID (or ISCI code), advertiser name, spot title, agency, audio configuration, and run time. Typically appears for 5 seconds.
  • Pre-Roll Black: 2 seconds of black and silent audio following the slate.
  • Active Commercial: Starts at an exact timecode (traditionally 01:00:00:00). The duration must match the exact bought time (e.g., 00:15:00, 00:30:00, 00:60:00) down to the frame.
  • Post-Roll Black: Networks typically require 0.5 to 2 seconds of silent black video at the end of the spot.
  • Safe Areas: Graphics, lower thirds, and legal disclaimers must stay within the standard 80% Title Safe and 90% Action Safe boundaries to prevent cut-off on varied television displays.

Quality Control (QC) and Delivery Portals

Before airing, commercials must clear Automated Quality Control (AQC) and human verification.

  • Photosensitive Epilepsy Testing: Commercials must comply with PSE standards (such as ITU-R BT.1702 or the Harding Flash and Pattern Analyzer) to ensure flashing elements do not induce seizures.
  • Ad-ID Compliance: Commercial assets in North America require a registered Ad-ID to track media assets through clearance, trafficking, and billing.
  • Delivery Platforms: Commercial files are rarely sent directly via FTP or email to individual stations. Instead, producers upload to centralized digital delivery aggregators—such as Extreme Reach, Peach, Comcast AdDelivery, or SpotGenie—which automate format transcoding, metadata verification, and delivery to targeted stations and networks.
