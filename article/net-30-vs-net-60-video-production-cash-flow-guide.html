Net 30 vs Net 60: Video Production Cash Flow Guide

Payment terms like Net 30 and Net 60 directly dictate when a video production business receives revenue versus when it must pay for project expenses. Because video production requires heavy upfront capital for crew, equipment rentals, location permits, and travel, waiting 30 to 60 days for payment creates a cash flow gap that can stall operations. Managing this gap requires understanding how deferred receivables interact with immediate liabilities, project turnaround times, and overall liquidity.

The Upfront Expense Mismatch

Video production operates on a front-loaded cost structure. Freelance crew members, gear rental houses, and catering vendors typically demand payment immediately or within seven to fourteen days of a shoot. In contrast, corporate clients and agencies routinely mandate Net 30 or Net 60 payment terms.

When a company accepts these terms, it essentially acts as an interest-free bank for the client. The production company pays out thousands of dollars during pre-production and production, but will not recover those costs until weeks or months after post-production is finished.

Net 30: A Manageable Balancing Act

Under Net 30 terms, a client agrees to pay the invoice within 30 days of receipt.

  • The Reality: Payment rarely arrives on day 30. The timeline from project kickoff to final payment usually spans 60 to 75 days because Net 30 begins only after the final video is approved and the invoice is submitted.
  • Cash Flow Impact: A production house with a healthy cash reserve can typically absorb a 30-day delay. It allows for predictable monthly forecasting, provided the business maintains a rolling reserve that covers at least two to three months of recurring operating expenses.

Net 60: High Risk and Working Capital Strain

Net 60 terms mean the client has 60 days from invoice submission to remit payment. This timeline poses severe cash flow challenges for video production businesses of any size.

  • Capital Lockup: When combined with a standard 30-day production schedule, a Net 60 term means the business carries project expenses for roughly 90 days. This ties up significant working capital, limiting the company’s ability to take on new projects simultaneously.
  • Vulnerability to Delays: If a client delays final video sign-off or if an accounting department processes the invoice late, a Net 60 invoice easily stretches into 75 to 90 days of waiting. A single late payment on a major project under Net 60 can jeopardize payroll, studio rent, and vendor relationships.

How to Protect Cash Flow Under Extended Terms

Video production companies can counter the risks of Net 30 and Net 60 arrangements by implementing specific structural safeguards:

  • Staggered Milestone Billing: Never tie the entire project fee to the final delivery. Standardize contracts with a 50% upfront deposit before pre-production begins, 25% upon completion of the shoot, and the remaining 25% upon final delivery. This ensures all out-of-pocket production costs are covered before post-production starts.
  • Early Payment Discounts: Incentivize faster turnaround by offering terms such as "2/10 Net 30," where the client receives a 2% discount if they pay within 10 days.
  • Clause-Based Invoicing: Start the payment clock earlier by tying invoices to specific calendar dates or project milestones rather than client sign-off, protecting the business from client-side review delays.
  • Surcharge for Net 60: Treat extended terms as credit financing. If a corporate client insists on Net 60, build a financing premium into the project quote to cover the cost of maintaining credit lines or floating the capital.
