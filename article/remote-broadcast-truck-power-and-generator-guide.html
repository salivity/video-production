Remote Broadcast Truck Power and Generator Guide

Remote broadcast vehicles require robust, clean, and continuous electrical infrastructure to prevent equipment failure and keep live transmissions on the air. Because outside broadcast (OB) environments combine high-draw mechanical loads with sensitive digital production electronics, engineers rely on a multi-stage power strategy. This article details the vital components required for remote broadcast power setups, including online uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), galvanic isolation transformers, voltage regulators, twin-pack generator configurations, and proper grounding techniques.

Power Conditioning Requirements

Sensitive video switchers, routing switchers, replay servers, and broadcast cameras cannot tolerate electrical noise, voltage fluctuations, or ground loops. Effective power conditioning for an OB truck must address three primary threats: noise, voltage swings, and transients.

  • Galvanic Isolation Transformers: Shore power at sports arenas or event venues is notoriously noisy and prone to ground differential issues. An onboard isolation transformer separates the venue's electrical ground from the truck’s technical ground, eliminating 60Hz hum in analog lines, preventing data packet loss on SDI/SMPTE cables, and blocking common-mode electrical noise.
  • Automatic Voltage Regulators (AVR): Venue voltages often sag during peak hours or surge when heavy machinery cycles on. Industrial-grade AVRs maintain a steady output voltage within a tight window (typically ±2% to ±3%), protecting internal power supplies from thermal stress and premature failure.
  • Surge Suppression (TVSS/SPD): Transient Voltage Surge Suppressors must be installed at the primary power entry point. These capture high-voltage spikes caused by lightning, heavy motor switching, or unstable grid connections before they reach interior distribution racks.

Double-Conversion Online UPS

A remote production unit cannot rely on consumer standby or line-interactive battery backups. The production demands a true double-conversion online UPS.

  • Zero Transfer Time: Online units continually rectify incoming AC power to DC, then invert that DC back into pristine, synthesized sine-wave AC power. Because the inverter is always supplying the load, there is a true zero-millisecond transfer time if the input power drops completely.
  • Technical vs. Utility Power Segregation: Broadcast trucks split power distribution into two distinct paths:
    • Technical Power (Clean): Fed directly through the UPS and isolation transformer to cameras, vision mixers, multiviewers, audio consoles, and transmission encoders.
    • Utility Power (Raw): Kept completely off the UPS circuit to power heavy inductive loads such as air conditioning, equipment rack ventilation, galley appliances, and hydraulic leveling jacks.

Generator Backup Architectures

Remote production typically operates off a mobile diesel generator either as the primary power source or as an immediate failsafe for unreliable shore power.

  • Twin-Pack (Dual Synchronized) Generators: The standard for major live sports and entertainment broadcasts is a dedicated truck or trailer housing two identical diesel generators. These units operate in parallel, sharing the load. If one generator suffers a mechanical or fuel failure, the surviving unit instantly assumes the full load without interrupting the broadcast.
  • Isochronous Electronic Governors: Standard construction generators fluctuate in engine speed (and therefore AC frequency) as electrical demand shifts. Broadcast generators require electronic isochronous governors to lock the frequency precisely at 60 Hz (or 50 Hz depending on the region), preventing timing errors in digital video sync generators and frame synchronizers.
  • Automatic Transfer Switches (ATS): An ATS with closed-transition capability allows the truck to switch between shore power and generator power without a momentary blackout, enabling seamless pre-show testing and controlled power source migration.

Grounding and Cam-Lok Interfacing

A dependable power system depends entirely on proper bonding and earthing at the venue:

  • Single-Point Star Grounding: All internal equipment racks and metallic chassis must bond back to a central technical ground point to avoid potential differences that generate ground loops and digital clocking jitter.
  • Earth Driven Rods: When running entirely on mobile generator power, a grounding conductor bonded directly to the vehicle chassis and the generator frame must be connected to an earth ground rod to ensure protective breakers trip properly in a fault condition.
  • Three-Phase Cam-Lok Infrastructure: Broadcast trucks typically ingest power via 400A or 200A three-phase, five-wire (Phase A, Phase B, Phase C, Neutral, Ground) series 16 Cam-Lok connections. Color-coded, locking cables prevent accidental disconnection and distribute the total wattage evenly across phases to minimize neutral current buildup.
