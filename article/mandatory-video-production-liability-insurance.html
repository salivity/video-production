Mandatory Video Production Liability Insurance

Operating a professional video production company involves significant physical, financial, and legal risks. While specific legal mandates vary by jurisdiction, securing film permits, renting locations, and hiring crew universally require certain commercial liability policies. This article outlines the essential liability insurance policies that are either legally mandated by state governments or contractually mandatory to conduct professional video shoots.

1. Commercial General Liability (CGL)

Commercial General Liability is contractually mandatory for nearly every commercial production. Municipalities will not issue film permits, and private property owners will not grant location access, without a Certificate of Insurance (COI) proving active CGL coverage.

  • What it covers: Third-party bodily injuries (e.g., a pedestrian tripping over a c-stand or cable) and third-party property damage (e.g., damaging flooring or walls at a rented filming location).
  • Standard requirements: Most film offices and corporate clients require a minimum limit of $1,000,000 per occurrence and a $2,000,000 aggregate, frequently requiring the location or client to be named as an "Additional Insured."

2. Workers' Compensation Insurance

Workers' Compensation is the primary insurance policy that is strictly mandated by law in almost every state and country once a production hires employees. Even if your crew consists of temporary freelancers or independent contractors, labor laws in many regions still classify them as statutory employees for the duration of the shoot.

  • What it covers: Medical expenses, rehabilitation costs, and lost wages if a cast or crew member is injured on the set or while traveling for production business.
  • Liability component: It includes Employer’s Liability coverage, which protects the production company if an injured worker sues for gross negligence.

3. Hired and Non-Owned Auto Liability (HNOA)

Standard personal auto policies exclude commercial activities, and production vehicles carry significant liability risks. Hired and Non-Owned Auto insurance is mandatory whenever a production rents picture cars, box trucks, or passenger vans, or when crew members drive their personal vehicles for production errands.

  • What it covers: Third-party bodily injury and property damage caused by vehicles rented by the production company (Hired) or personal vehicles driven on behalf of the company (Non-Owned).
  • Requirements: Rental houses routinely refuse to release production vehicles without proof of this coverage on file.

4. Third-Party Property Damage Liability

While basic General Liability covers standard third-party property damage, it often contains exclusions for property under the production's direct "care, custody, and control." Third-Party Property Damage Liability is an extension—often bundled into a broader inland marine or production insurance package—that is mandatory when renting high-end filming venues.

  • What it covers: Accidental damage caused directly to the real property you are renting or occupying exclusively for filming, such as studio spaces, residential filming locations, or historical buildings.

Securing a Production Insurance Package

Rather than purchasing these policies separately, video production companies typically secure a specialized Film and Production Insurance package. These packages can be obtained on an annual basis for ongoing production companies or as a short-term policy for single projects, ensuring all regulatory and contractual liability requirements are satisfied before cameras roll.
