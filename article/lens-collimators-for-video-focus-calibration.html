Lens Collimators for Video Focus Calibration

Lens collimators are precision optical instruments used during camera prep to verify that lenses achieve sharp focus across their entire range, particularly at infinity. In major video productions, focus errors cannot be fixed in post-production, making mechanical and optical verification essential before shooting begins. This article explores how collimators work, why they are indispensable for testing flange focal distance and witness marks, and how they protect high-budget productions from costly optical errors.

Simulating Infinity in Confined Spaces

A lens collimator projects a test reticle through a specialized optical system that emits parallel light rays. This optical design mimics a target positioned at optical infinity. In a rental house or prep bay, space is typically limited, making physical distance testing at infinity impossible. A collimator allows technicians and camera assistants (focus pullers) to critically inspect optical infinity within a footprint of just a few feet.

Verifying and Adjusting Flange Focal Distance (Back Focus)

The primary role of a lens collimator is checking the flange focal distance—the exact mechanical distance from the rear lens mount to the sensor or film plane. If a lens's back focus is off by even a fraction of a millimeter:

  • Wide-angle lenses will fail to focus sharply.
  • Distance scales (witness marks) on the barrel will not align with physical measurements.
  • Zoom lenses will lose focus as focal lengths change (loss of parfocality).

Technicians place the lens on a dedicated collimator test bench to inspect the sharpness of the projected reticle. If the infinity mark does not cleanly resolve when set to mechanical infinity, the lens must be re-shimmed. Shims are ultra-thin brass or plastic rings placed between the lens mount and the lens body to calibrate the spacing with micron-level precision.

Calibrating Witness Marks for Focus Pullers

Modern cinema workflows rely heavily on 1st Camera Assistants pulling focus by distance measurements rather than relying solely on onboard monitors. If the physical distance markers stamped on the lens barrel do not match the real-world focal plane, critical shots will fall out of focus during live takes. Collimators verify that infinity actually corresponds to the infinity symbol on the barrel, ensuring that all subsequent intermediate distance witness marks remain mathematically accurate.

Ensuring Parfocal Consistency in Cinema Zooms

True cinema zoom lenses are designed to be parfocal, maintaining crisp focus as the camera operator zooms in or out. Over time, mechanical wear, transport vibrations, and temperature swings can disrupt this alignment. A collimator tests the zoom's front and rear focal elements simultaneously, ensuring that focus holds stable across the entire zoom range.

Mitigating Financial and Schedule Risks

On major film and commercial sets, production time costs thousands of dollars per minute. Discovering back focus issues, soft images, or mismatched multi-camera setups on day one of production can bring a shoot to a halt or necessitate expensive reshoots. Using a lens collimator during camera prep guarantees that every piece of glass mounted to the camera performs predictably and flawlessly before the crew ever steps onto the set.
