Building a Custom PC for 8K Video Editing

Building a custom workstation for 8K video production requires balancing raw compute performance, extreme memory bandwidth, and high-speed data pipelines. This guide breaks down the essential hardware components you need to handle massive 8K RAW video streams, complex timelines, and multi-layered effects without frame drops or rendering bottlenecks.

High-Core-Count CPU with Strong Single-Thread Performance

The central processing unit dictates how efficiently your software decodes footage, applies CPU-based effects, and exports finished timelines. For 8K video, your CPU must balance high core counts with fast single-core clock speeds:

  • Core Count: Aim for a minimum of 16 cores and 32 threads (such as an AMD Ryzen 9 or Intel Core i9). For complex multi-cam workflows and multi-layer rendering, consider workstation-grade platforms like AMD Threadripper with 24 to 64 cores.
  • Architecture: Modern architectures feature hardware decoders for formats like HEVC and ProRes, significantly easing timeline playback.

GPU with High Video Memory (VRAM)

Modern Non-Linear Editors (NLEs) such as DaVinci Resolve and Adobe Premiere Pro rely heavily on the GPU for real-time effects, color grading, noise reduction, and AI-accelerated tasks. In 8K workflows, GPU VRAM capacity is the primary limiter.

  • VRAM Capacity: Running out of VRAM causes instant timeline stutters or software crashes. A minimum of 16 GB of VRAM is required, though 24 GB (such as an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090) is strongly recommended to handle native 8K frames and heavy color nodes.
  • Multi-GPU Scaling: Some NLEs, particularly DaVinci Resolve Studio, scale effectively with dual GPUs, allowing one card to focus on display output while the other processes compute-heavy tasks.

System Memory (RAM) Capacity and Speed

8K frames contain four times as many pixels as 4K, meaning timeline previews and frame caches consume enormous amounts of memory.

  • Capacity: 64 GB is the absolute bare minimum for basic 8K cuts. For professional editing, multi-track timelines, or working with After Effects, 128 GB of RAM is the standard baseline, with 256 GB recommended for high-end production suites.
  • Speed and Bandwidth: Opt for DDR5 memory configured in multi-channel modes (dual-channel for consumer platforms, quad-channel or eight-channel for workstation platforms) to avoid data bottlenecks between the CPU and memory pool.

Multi-Drive NVMe Storage Architecture

Handling multi-gigabyte-per-second 8K bitrates requires an optimized storage layout rather than a single drive. Segregate your storage across distinct PCIe NVMe solid-state drives:

  • Operating System and Applications: A dedicated 1 TB PCIe 4.0 NVMe SSD.
  • Scratch Disk and Media Cache: A dedicated 1 TB to 2 TB high-endurance NVMe drive for temporary cache, peak files, and render scratch pads.
  • Active Project Storage: High-capacity PCIe 4.0 or 5.0 NVMe drives configured in RAID 0, or high-speed direct-attached storage (DAS), delivering sustained read and write speeds of at least 3,000 to 5,000 MB/s.

Motherboard Connectivity and Expansion

An 8K workstation requires extensive I/O to transfer massive raw footage files and connect to external storage arrays:

  • Thunderbolt 4 / USB4: Essential for high-speed direct-attached RAID storage and ingest card readers.
  • High-Speed Networking: A built-in 10 GbE (Gigabit Ethernet) port is mandatory if you edit directly from or offload to a Network Attached Storage (NAS) environment.
  • PCIe Lanes: Ensure the motherboard provides enough PCIe lanes to run your primary GPU, high-speed storage, and any capture or I/O cards (such as Blackmagic DeckLink) at their full bandwidth without lane sharing.

Power Supply and Cooling Stability

Sustained 8K rendering pushes the CPU and GPU to maximum load for hours at a time, making stability and thermal management critical:

  • Cooling: Use a high-performance 360mm or 420mm liquid AIO cooler, or a premium dual-tower air cooler, housed inside a high-airflow chassis with plenty of intake and exhaust fans to avoid thermal throttling.
  • Power Supply Unit (PSU): Choose a reputable, fully modular PSU rated at 1000W to 1300W with an 80 Plus Gold or Platinum certification to deliver stable power under prolonged peak loads.
