Camera Cheese Plate Utility in Video Production

A camera cheese plate is a flat, durable metal plate perforated with a multi-hole grid of threaded mounting points, serving as a universal foundation for attaching miscellaneous accessories to a camera rig. In video production, cheese plates solve the problem of limited native camera mounting space by providing a modular, secure platform for power systems, wireless transmitters, audio receivers, and monitoring equipment. This guide breaks down the practical utility of cheese plates and why they are essential for professional video workflows.

Universal Mounting Points

Modern camera bodies typically feature only a few 1/4"-20 or 3/8"-16 threaded sockets, which quickly run out when building a professional setup. A cheese plate features dozens of standard threaded holes—often mixed with ARRI locating pin holes—allowing you to mount multiple accessories simultaneously. This turns an otherwise cramped setup into an organized, fully functional production rig without overcrowding the camera body.

Secure Mounting for Heavy Accessories

High-draw cinema cameras and accessories require substantial power. Cheese plates are the industry-standard solution for mounting V-mount or Gold-mount battery adapter plates. Because cheese plates are precision-machined from solid aluminum, they handle the leverage and weight of heavy broadcast batteries without flexing, stripping, or detaching during movement.

Better Weight Distribution and Balance

Rig balance is critical for shoulder-mount setups, gimbals, and tripod operation. Cheese plates allow operators to shift accessory weight toward the rear, top, or sides of the rig. By mounting heavy components like batteries, counterweights, or wireless systems further back via a cheese plate, you can balance front-heavy cinema lenses and minimize operator fatigue.

Protection of Native Camera Threads

Directly screwing and unscrewing metal arms, clamps, and brackets into the camera’s internal chassis risks stripping the threads over time. A cheese plate acts as an intermediary sacrificial layer. If a thread strips due to cross-threading or excessive torque, replacing an inexpensive cheese plate is far cheaper and faster than sending a camera body to a service center for repairs.

Clean Cable Management and Organization

Loose cables introduce failure points, such as snagging or port damage. Many cheese plates accommodate dedicated cable clamps or pass-through cutouts that secure HDMI, SDI, and power cables firmly in place. By grouping auxiliary receivers and power distribution boxes (such as D-Tap splitters) onto a single plate, wiring remains tidy and accessible.

Modular and Fast Transitions

Production environments require quick adaptations between shooting modes—such as moving from a tripod to a shoulder rig or handheld gimbal. By mounting an entire accessory ecosystem (audio receivers, wireless video, power) to a single cheese plate, operators can detach or reposition the entire cluster in seconds, drastically reducing downtime on set.
