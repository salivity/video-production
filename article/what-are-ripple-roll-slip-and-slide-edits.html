What Are Ripple, Roll, Slip, and Slide Edits?

Fine-tuning a video timeline requires precise adjustments to clip timing, transitions, and sequence pacing without breaking the overall sync. Ripple, roll, slip, and slide edits represent the four primary dynamic trimming operations used across professional non-linear editing (NLE) software. Mastering these tools allows editors to manipulate in-points, out-points, and clip placement seamlessly, cutting down manual timeline repositioning and preserving the rhythmic flow of a project.

The Ripple Edit: Adjusting Length and Ripple-Shifting the Timeline

A ripple edit changes the duration of a specific clip by moving either its in-point or out-point, while automatically shifting all subsequent media along the timeline.

When you perform a standard trim using a selection tool, shortening a clip creates a gap (empty space), and lengthening it overwrites adjacent media. A ripple edit prevents gaps and accidental overwrites:

  • Shortening a clip: Downstream clips pull forward to close the empty space automatically.
  • Lengthening a clip: Downstream clips push backward to make room for the added frames.

Because downstream assets move in unison, a ripple edit changes the total duration of the entire sequence. It is the primary tool for removing unwanted pauses, cleaning up interview takes, or extending a reaction shot without leaving blank spaces on the tracks.

The Roll Edit: Shifting the Cut Point Between Adjacent Clips

A rolling edit (or dual-roller trim) adjusts the boundary between two adjacent clips simultaneously without altering the overall sequence duration.

When performing a roll edit, you drag the shared cut point (the edit point) between Clip A and Clip B:

  • Moving the cut point forward extends the out-point of Clip A while trimming the in-point of Clip B by the exact same number of frames.
  • Moving the cut point backward trims Clip A and extends Clip B.

Because one clip gains the exact amount of screen time the other loses, the position of everything else on the timeline remains locked. Roll edits are essential for matching cuts to music beats, pacing dialogue exchanges, and refining reaction timing.

The Slip Edit: Changing the Media Range Inside Fixed Boundaries

A slip edit changes the starting and ending frames visible within a clip while keeping its placement and duration on the timeline completely unchanged.

When slipping a clip:

  • The clip's in-point and out-point in the source media shift forward or backward by the same amount.
  • The position on the sequence track and the neighboring clips remain untouched.
  • The total duration of both the clip and the overall sequence stays the same.

This operation requires available source material (handles) before the in-point or after the out-point. Slip edits are ideal when a shot is positioned perfectly in the rhythm of the cut, but a specific visual element—such as a gesture, expression, or graphic—needs to happen earlier or later within that allocated window.

The Slide Edit: Repositioning a Clip Between Its Neighbors

A slide edit moves an entire clip earlier or later along the timeline while maintaining its internal in/out points and total duration. To accommodate the movement, the adjacent clips automatically adjust.

During a slide edit:

  • The selected middle clip moves left or right along the timeline without changing its content or duration.
  • The preceding clip's out-point trims or extends to track with the moving clip.
  • The succeeding clip's in-point trims or extends inversely to fill the space.

The sequence length remains fixed. Editors use slide edits when a specific cutaway or B-roll shot must appear at a slightly different moment in time relative to the surrounding background footage or audio track.

Summary of Editing Behaviors

Edit Type Clip In/Out Content Clip Placement on Track Adjacent Clips Affected Sequence Length
Ripple In or Out changes Remains at start point Shifted earlier or later Changes
Roll Out of A, In of B change Shared cut point moves Both adjacent clips trim Unchanged
Slip Both In and Out change Stays fixed Unaffected Unchanged
Slide Stays fixed Moves earlier or later Previous and next clips trim Unchanged

Selecting the appropriate trim tool eliminates the need to manually cut, delete, close gaps, or realign multiple tracks. Utilizing ripple, roll, slip, and slide commands ensures precise micro-adjustments while maintaining track synchronization throughout post-production.
