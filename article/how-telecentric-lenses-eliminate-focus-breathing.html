How Telecentric Lenses Eliminate Focus Breathing

Focus breathing—the distracting shift in magnification and framing that occurs when changing focus—can break immersion during dramatic video rack focus shots. This article explains how telecentric optical design eliminates this artifact by aligning the system’s chief rays parallel to the optical axis. By maintaining a constant image scale regardless of focal distance, telecentric systems allow cinematographers to execute seamless, high-stakes focus pulls without the perception of optical zooming.

In standard photographic lenses, moving focus elements alters the lens's internal geometry and pupil positions. As focus shifts from the foreground to the background, the angle of the light cones hitting the camera sensor changes, causing the image magnification to expand or contract. In narrative cinematography, this manifests as an unnatural zooming sensation that distracts viewers from the emotional or dramatic weight of a scene.

Telecentricity solves this issue through optical geometry. In an image-space telecentric lens, the optical system is designed so that the exit pupil is located at infinity. Because the pupil is at infinity, the chief rays (the central rays of each imaging pencil of light) exit the rear element parallel to the optical axis across the entire sensor plane, rather than spreading out obliquely toward the edges.

When a camera operator racks focus using an image-space telecentric design, the blur circles of out-of-focus objects expand and contract symmetrically along the optical axis. Because the chief rays are strictly parallel, the centers of these blur circles do not shift laterally across the sensor. Consequently, the magnification of the scene remains identical whether an object is tack-sharp or heavily defocused.

In conventional lenses, a defocused background object physically changes size on the frame as focus transitions toward it. In a telecentric system, the object simply transitions from a soft blur to sharp focus while occupying the exact same screen real estate throughout the entire rotation of the focus ring.

Implementing telecentricity in video production lenses requires complex optical designs, often involving larger glass elements to accommodate the parallel ray paths across large-format cinema sensors. Despite these physical demands, cinema lens manufacturers utilize image-space telecentric configurations—often paired with floating internal focus groups—to ensure that dramatic rack focus transitions remain invisible, preserving frame composition and narrative immersion.
