Should You Film Commercials in Studio or on Location?

Choosing between a dedicated studio and an on-location shoot is a pivotal pre-production decision that dictates your commercial's visual style, budget, logistical complexity, and production timeline. While studio environments provide total control over lighting, sound, and weather, location shoots offer unmatched real-world authenticity and built-in production value. Deciding which route to take requires evaluating key creative, financial, and logistical factors to align with your project goals.

Environmental Control and Predictability

Studio shoots excel when precision and consistency are essential. In a soundstage, production teams control every variable:

  • Lighting: Complete elimination of shifting sun angles, clouds, or inconsistent natural daylight.
  • Acoustics: Sound-treated walls prevent interference from traffic, aircraft, construction, and sirens.
  • Weather: Zero risk of rain, wind, extreme temperatures, or sudden weather delays that can derail a shooting schedule.

Location shoots introduce external variables that require contingency plans, such as booking weather-cover indoor scenes or budgeting for automated dialogue replacement (ADR) in post-production.

Authenticity vs. Creative Flexibility

The creative vision of the commercial often indicates the appropriate setting:

  • Real-World Texture: On-location shoots provide organic textures, natural depth, and genuine architectural details that can be costly or difficult to replicate on a set.
  • Customization and Staging: Studios allow for stylized aesthetics, custom-built sets, seamless cyclorama wall transitions, and green screen or virtual production setups that would be impossible in existing physical spaces.

Budget Allocation and Permitting

Cost structures differ significantly between the two approaches:

Cost Factor Studio Production On-Location Production
Space Access Fixed daily stage rental rate Location fees, property owner agreements
Permits & Legal Covered by standard facility operating permits City/municipal film permits, street closures, police escorts
Logistics Built-in amenities, dressing rooms, grid power Generator rentals, basecamp setup, portable restrooms
Art Department Higher spend on set construction and dressing Lower build costs; uses existing architecture

Crew Size, Gear, and Cast Logistics

Logistical scope heavily influences location viability. Moving high volumes of specialized camera equipment, large crews, and talent to a remote or public site increases transport costs, setup times, and parking requirements. Studios offer drive-in loading bays, permanent rigging grids, dedicated hair and makeup suites, and stable high-amperage power, allowing large technical crews to operate efficiently.

Brand Identity and Story Context

The product itself often dictates the choice. High-end product showcases, cosmetics, and technical demonstrations benefit from the clinical perfection and macro-lighting control of a studio. Conversely, lifestyle campaigns, automotive ads, hospitality features, and community-driven narratives rely on the emotional connection and realism generated by genuine environments.
