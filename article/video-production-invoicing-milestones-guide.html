Video Production Invoicing Milestones Guide

Structuring invoicing milestones across pre-production, filming, and post-production ensures consistent cash flow for the creator and reduces financial risk for the client. Rather than relying on a single lump-sum payment at completion, standard industry practice divides the total project cost into distinct payments tied to tangible phase completions. This guide breaks down the standard invoicing milestones used across the video production lifecycle, common payment split models, and critical terms to include in your agreements.

Milestone 1: Pre-Production (The Upfront Deposit)

The initial invoice is issued upon contract signing, before any creative or logistical work begins.

  • Typical Amount: 33% to 50% of the total project cost.
  • Trigger: Contract execution and project kickoff.
  • Purpose: Covers upfront, non-refundable expenses such as scriptwriting, storyboarding, location scouting, permits, casting, and securing crew and equipment rentals.
  • Key Condition: Work should not commence until this initial payment clears.

Milestone 2: Production (Principal Photography)

The second milestone is tied to the physical filming phase. Depending on the size of the project, this payment is invoiced either right before cameras roll or immediately after filming wraps.

  • Typical Amount: 25% to 33% of the total project cost.
  • Trigger: The first day of principal photography or the completion of the shoot (production wrap).
  • Purpose: Covers day rates for crew, talent fees, equipment return costs, and on-set incidentals.
  • Key Condition: For high-risk or multi-day shoots, many producers require this payment before footage is shot to protect against last-minute client cancellations or delays.

Milestone 3: Post-Production (The Rough Cut / First Review)

In four-part payment structures, an intermediate milestone occurs during editing. This stage confirms that the project is on track creatively before final color grading, sound mixing, and visual effects are locked.

  • Typical Amount: 15% to 25% of the total project cost.
  • Trigger: Delivery of the first assembled rough cut for client review.
  • Purpose: Funds the editorial phase, including music licensing, voiceover recording, and motion graphics.
  • Key Condition: Delivery should always be watermarked with timecodes to prevent unauthorized use before final payment.

Milestone 4: Final Delivery (Sign-off and Asset Release)

The final invoice is triggered when post-production revisions are complete and the client approves the master video.

  • Typical Amount: 10% to 25% of the total project cost (or the remaining balance).
  • Trigger: Formal approval of the final cut, prior to the handoff of unwatermarked, full-resolution master files.
  • Purpose: Closes out the project, covers administrative margins, and serves as the final release of all agreed-upon deliverables.
  • Key Condition: High-resolution video files, raw footage, and project archives should strictly be withheld until this balance is settled in full.

Common Invoicing Percentage Structures

Depending on the scale and duration of the video project, producers typically select one of three standard milestone structures:

  • The 50/25/25 Model (Standard Commercial):
    • 50% deposit upon contract signing.
    • 25% upon completion of filming.
    • 25% upon final approval and delivery of master files.
  • The 33/33/34 Model (Balanced Long-Term):
    • 33.3% deposit at project kickoff.
    • 33.3% upon wrap of principal photography.
    • 33.4% upon final master delivery.
  • The 50/50 Model (Small or Rapid Projects):
    • 50% deposit upon signing.
    • 50% upon final master delivery.

Essential Practices for Milestone Billing

  1. Tie Milestones to Objective Events: Avoid vague triggers like "client satisfaction." Instead, tie milestones to clear deliverables (e.g., "Delivery of V1 Rough Cut" or "Completion of Day 2 Filming").
  2. Include a Kill Fee Clause: Define what percentage of the project fee is retained if the client cancels during pre-production or post-production.
  3. Limit Revision Rounds: Tie milestone sign-offs to a strict number of revision rounds (typically two rounds of edits) to prevent scope creep before the final balance is paid.
  4. Watermark Review Copies: Always deliver preview links with prominent watermarks and timecodes until the final invoice is paid.
