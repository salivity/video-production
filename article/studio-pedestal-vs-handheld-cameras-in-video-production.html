Studio Pedestal vs Handheld Cameras in Video Production

In professional video production, selecting the right camera system depends heavily on the shooting environment, movement requirements, and broadcast workflow. Studio pedestal cameras and handheld field units serve fundamentally different purposes: pedestals offer unmatched mechanical stability, precision, and multi-camera control for controlled live broadcasts, while handheld units prioritize mobility, rapid deployment, and operational independence in unpredictable, on-location settings.

Form Factor and Weight

Studio pedestal setups are massive, often weighing between 100 and 300 pounds when fully assembled with counterweights, teleprompters, large viewfinders, and heavy zoom lenses. They are engineered to stay planted on smooth studio floors.

Handheld field units—often referred to as Electronic News Gathering (ENG) or cinema rigs—are lightweight and compact, typically weighing between 5 and 20 pounds fully rigged. They are designed to be shoulder-mounted, handheld, or rigged to stabilizers like gimbals and EasyRigs.

Movement and Support Systems

  • Studio Pedestals: Use pneumatic or hydraulic vertical columns mounted on heavy wheeled bases (dollies). Operators can raise, lower, pan, and tilt the camera simultaneously with fluid, vibration-free precision using steering wheels and pan bars. They require flat, level surfaces.
  • Handheld Units: Rely on the operator’s body for positioning. Operators can walk, run, weave through crowds, and quickly change angles from ground-level to overhead, making them ideal for dynamic, fast-paced environments where stationary rigs cannot go.

Optics and Lenses

Studio cameras are typically outfitted with large "box lenses" that offer extreme optical zoom ratios (often 50x to 100x or more) with large apertures. Because the pedestal bears the weight, optical elements do not need to be lightweight. Focus, zoom, and iris are controlled remotely via mechanical or digital demands mounted on the pan handles.

Field units utilize compact broadcast or cinema lenses designed for minimal weight and physical balance. While they have lower zoom ranges (typically 10x to 25x for ENG lenses), they allow manual ring operation directly on the lens barrel, enabling faster, intuitive adjustments on the go.

Signal Routing, Power, and Control

  • Studio Pedestals: Rarely record internally. Instead, they send uncompressed video signals to a control room via SMPTE fiber or triax cables. These cables also deliver power, return video, tally lights, and two-way intercom to the operator. Camera settings (exposure, color balance, gamma) are controlled remotely by a video engineer using a Camera Control Unit (CCU).
  • Handheld Units: Primarily operate as standalone systems. They feature internal recording media (such as CFexpress or SD cards), onboard battery power (V-Mount or Gold-Mount), and optional wireless transmitters to send video back to a base station. The camera operator is responsible for both camera movement and camera settings.

Production Environment

Studio pedestals are standard equipment for newscasts, talk shows, sitcoms, and indoor live events where visual consistency, smooth tracking shots, and synchronized multi-camera switching are essential.

Handheld field units are the standard for news reporting, sports sideline coverage, documentaries, and reality television, where agility, quick setup times, and adaptability to uneven terrain are necessary.
