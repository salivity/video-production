Storage Bandwidth for Multi-Stream RAW Video Editing

Editing uncompressed multi-stream RAW video places extreme performance demands on shared and local storage architectures. This article breaks down the mathematical data rates of uncompressed and raw footage, calculates total sustained throughput requirements for concurrent multi-camera timelines, and identifies the hardware interfaces and drive configurations needed to avoid dropped frames during real-time multi-stream playback.

Understanding the Raw Data Rates

To calculate storage bandwidth, you must distinguish between uncompressed RGB files (such as DPX or TIFF sequences) and single-sensor uncompressed Bayer RAW.

For single-sensor uncompressed RAW, the bit depth directly scales with resolution and frame rate:

\[\text{Data Rate (bps)} = \text{Horizontal Pixels} \times \text{Vertical Pixels} \times \text{Bit Depth} \times \text{Frames Per Second}\]

For uncompressed RGB 4:4:4 formats, each pixel contains three discrete color channels, effectively tripling the data load:

\[\text{Data Rate (bps)} = \text{Horizontal Pixels} \times \text{Vertical Pixels} \times \text{Bit Depth} \times 3 \times \text{Frames Per Second}\]

Baseline Single-Stream Bandwidth (at 24 fps, 12-bit):

  • 4K UHD (3840 × 2160) Uncompressed Bayer RAW: ~298 MB/s (2.38 Gbps)
  • 4K UHD (3840 × 2160) Uncompressed 4:4:4 RGB: ~895 MB/s (7.16 Gbps)
  • 8K DCI (8192 × 4320) Uncompressed Bayer RAW: ~1.27 GB/s (10.16 Gbps)
  • 8K DCI (8192 × 4320) Uncompressed 4:4:4 RGB: ~3.82 GB/s (30.56 Gbps)

At higher frame rates (such as 60 fps or 120 fps), these base figures multiply by 2.5× and 5× respectively.

Multi-Stream Bandwidth Calculations

Multi-stream editing occurs during multi-camera switching, picture-in-picture composites, real-time transitions, and background rendering while scrubbing. Production pipelines typically require concurrent playback of 3 to 4 streams at minimum, scaling to 9 or 16 streams for studio multicam.

1. Standard 3-Stream Editing (A/B Roll with Transitions)

  • 4K 12-bit RAW @ 24 fps: ~894 MB/s sustained
  • 4K 12-bit RGB @ 24 fps: ~2.68 GB/s sustained
  • 8K 12-bit RAW @ 24 fps: ~3.81 GB/s sustained
  • 8K 12-bit RGB @ 24 fps: ~11.46 GB/s sustained

2. Four-Camera (4-Stream) Multicam Sync

  • 4K 12-bit RAW @ 24 fps: ~1.19 GB/s sustained
  • 4K 12-bit RGB @ 24 fps: ~3.58 GB/s sustained
  • 8K 12-bit RAW @ 24 fps: ~5.08 GB/s sustained
  • 8K 12-bit RGB @ 24 fps: ~15.28 GB/s sustained

3. High Frame Rate (4-Stream Multicam @ 60 fps)

  • 4K 12-bit RAW @ 60 fps: ~2.98 GB/s sustained
  • 8K 12-bit RAW @ 60 fps: ~12.70 GB/s sustained

Storage Headroom and I/O Performance Requirements

Streaming video data is not strictly linear. File systems introduce metadata overhead, and Non-Linear Editors (NLEs) make out-of-order block requests when reading multiple independent files simultaneously.

  • The 25–30% Headroom Rule: To prevent dropped frames, calculate maximum throughput with a 25% to 30% safety margin above the raw data rate. For example, a 4-stream 8K RAW workflow requiring 5.08 GB/s demands a storage system capable of at least 6.6 GB/s of sustained, non-burst read performance.
  • IOPS and Latency: While streaming bandwidth measures raw volume, multi-stream playback turns sequential access into quasi-random access. Storage arrays must deliver low read latency (sub-millisecond) to concurrently serve multiple file pointers across the disk array.

Infrastructure and Interconnect Requirements

Traditional 1GbE and 10GbE networking, as well as spinning-disk (HDD) RAID setups, are insufficient for multi-stream uncompressed workflows.

  • Drive Media: SATA SSDs in RAID arrays can service 4K multi-stream RAW up to roughly 2 to 3 GB/s. For 8K multi-stream or 4K RGB multi-stream setups, NVMe U.2/U.3 SSDs configured in PCIe Gen 4 or Gen 5 RAID arrays are required to hit the 6 to 16+ GB/s sustained transfer rates.
  • Workstation Interconnects:
    • Direct-attached storage requires Thunderbolt 4 (capped at roughly 3 GB/s real-world read) for light 4K work, or direct PCIe-over-cable / dual-Thunderbolt arrays for higher demands.
    • Network Attached Storage (NAS) or Storage Area Networks (SAN) require 25GbE (yielding ~2.7 GB/s real-world), 40GbE/50GbE (~4.5 to 5.5 GB/s), or 100GbE (~11.5 GB/s) per client, or multi-channel 32G/64G Fibre Channel fabrics.
