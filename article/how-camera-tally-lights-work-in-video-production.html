How Camera Tally Lights Work in Video Production

Tally light systems are essential signaling devices used in live multi-camera broadcast production to show on-air talent and crew which camera is actively broadcasting. Controlled directly by the production switcher, these visual indicators eliminate guesswork in fast-paced studio environments by illuminating specific colors that signify whether a camera is live, cued for next use, or inactive. This article breaks down how tally systems operate, how visual signals are transmitted, and how talent reads these cues during live broadcasts.

The Signal Path: From Switcher to Camera

The tally workflow originates at the production switcher (vision mixer), which the technical director uses to switch between visual feeds.

  1. Triggering the Signal: When the director cuts to or previews a camera, the switcher assigns that input to either the "Program" (live) or "Preview" bus.
  2. Transmission: The switcher sends a tally control signal to the camera setup. This communication happens through several methods:
    • Contact Closure (GPI/GPO): Physical relay contacts closing a circuit between the switcher and camera system.
    • Ethernet/IP Networks: Modern IP protocols (such as NDI, TSL UMD, or proprietary IP protocols) transmitting metadata packets over local networks.
    • Embedded SDI Data: Tally data embedded alongside audio, video, and timecode signals directly over standard SDI or optical fiber cables.
  3. Receipt and Display: The signal reaches the Camera Control Unit (CCU) in the control room or directly arrives at the camera head, triggering the illumination of specific tally LEDs.

Tally Placement for Talent

For on-air talent, looking into the correct camera lens is vital for maintaining a connection with the viewing audience. Because studio floors are busy with multiple moving cameras, tally lights are positioned directly in the talent’s natural field of view:

  • Lens Hood or Matte Box: A forward-facing LED light sits immediately above or beneath the camera lens.
  • Teleprompter Bezels: In news and scripted environments, tally LEDs are installed directly onto the teleprompter frame, ensuring the talent sees the light without shifting their gaze away from the script.
  • Talent Monitors: Large floor monitors (often called talent or confidence monitors) often display on-screen tally borders around the return program feed.

Universal Color Coding

Tally lights rely on standardized color schemes so talent and camera operators can interpret camera states instantly:

  • Red (Program / On-Air): The camera is currently live and broadcasting to the public or being recorded to master. When an anchor sees a red tally, they must direct their speech and eye contact toward that lens.
  • Green or Amber (Preview / Next): The camera is cued on the switcher's preview bus and will likely be taken live next. This gives talent a brief moment to prepare their posture, shift eye contact smoothly, and anticipate the next shot.
  • Unlit (Standby): The camera is not in use or is undergoing repositioning by the operator.

Ensuring Seamless Broadcasts

By translating switcher decisions into immediate visual cues, tally systems prevent anchors from addressing the wrong lens, allow smooth transitions between speakers, and keep camera operators informed without cluttering operational intercom channels with verbal warnings.
